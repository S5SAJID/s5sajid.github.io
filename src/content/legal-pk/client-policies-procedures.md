> **Purpose of This Document:** This document governs how we work together on a day-to-day basis. It is not a substitute for the Master Services Agreement (MSA) — it is an operational guide that ensures both parties understand what to expect at every stage of a project. All clients are required to read and accept this document before work commences. In case of conflict with the MSA, the MSA prevails.



## SECTION 1: WORKING HOURS & AVAILABILITY

### 1.1 Standard Working Hours

The Provider is available for active development, communication, and support during the following hours only:

**Monday through Friday**
9:00 AM — 6:00 PM Pakistan Standard Time (PST / UTC+5)

**Saturday, Sunday, and Public Holidays**
Not available unless a prior written arrangement has been made and confirmed.

### 1.2 Extended Availability

On certain days, the Provider may be available until 9:00 PM PKT based on workload and personal schedule. This extended availability is not guaranteed and shall not be relied upon by the Client as a standard expectation.

### 1.3 After-Hours Communication Policy

Messages received outside of working hours — regardless of medium (WhatsApp, email, phone, or otherwise) — will be acknowledged and responded to on the next Working Day. The Client shall not expect, demand, or assume availability outside the hours stated in Section 1.1.

**Critical clarification for Pakistani clients:** The Provider is a sole professional, not a support team or a 24/7 service. Sending repeated messages outside working hours, calling repeatedly, or expressing frustration at non-response outside working hours constitutes a violation of these policies and may result in termination of the engagement.

### 1.4 Holiday Notice

The Provider will provide reasonable advance notice (minimum 3 Working Days) of planned absences, holidays, or periods of unavailability that may impact project timelines.

### 1.5 Emergency Protocol

For genuine production emergencies (as defined under "Serious Bug" in the MSA, Article 1.10), the Client may send a clearly marked emergency message outside working hours. The Provider will make reasonable efforts to acknowledge such messages within four (4) hours. Resolution of the emergency will occur within the next Working Day unless complexity requires more time.



## SECTION 2: COMMUNICATION PROTOCOLS

### 2.1 Accepted Communication Channels

The Provider accepts and responds to communications through the following channels:

| Channel | Purpose | Response Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Email** | Formal communications, contracts, scope changes, invoices | Within 24 hours on Working Days |
| **WhatsApp** | Quick updates, status checks, urgent questions | Same day during working hours |
| **Project Management Tool** (Asana, Notion, or agreed tool) | Task tracking, milestone management | Within 24 hours on Working Days |

### 2.2 Channel Hierarchy

- **Legal and scope matters** (changes, disputes, complaints, approvals): **Email only.** WhatsApp messages do not constitute binding approval for scope changes, additional features, or contract amendments unless confirmed via email.
- **Day-to-day progress updates:** WhatsApp is acceptable.
- **Invoices and payment confirmations:** Email only.

### 2.3 Client Response Time Obligation

The Client must respond to the Provider's requests — including requests for feedback, approvals, content, credentials, or decisions — within **three (3) Working Days.** This obligation is not optional; it is a contractual requirement.

Consequences of non-response are as follows:

| Duration of Non-Response | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **3 Working Days** | Provider sends formal written reminder |
| **5 Working Days** | Project officially paused; Provider may allocate time to other clients |
| **7+ Working Days** | Project timeline extended by 1 week per week of delay; no liability on Provider |
| **21 Consecutive Calendar Days** | Project treated as abandoned; deposit forfeited; work cancelled |

### 2.4 Quality of Communication

To ensure efficient delivery, the Client is expected to:

- Provide feedback that is specific and actionable (e.g., "The login button on mobile is not visible" rather than "something looks wrong")
- Consolidate feedback into a single communication rather than multiple fragmented messages
- Distinguish between bugs (something broken) and preferences (something the Client wants changed) — the two are handled differently and billed differently

### 2.5 Prohibited Communication Conduct

The following communication conduct is strictly prohibited and may result in immediate termination of the engagement:

- Abusive, threatening, or disrespectful language in any form
- Repeated messages within a short time span demanding immediate responses
- Contacting the Provider at late hours with non-emergency matters
- Circumventing communication channels to apply social pressure (e.g., contacting the Provider's family, social media, or workplace connections)



## SECTION 3: PROJECT PHASES & WORKFLOW

Every project follows a structured workflow. Understanding this process prevents delays and sets accurate expectations.

### Phase 1: Discovery & Scope Definition

**What happens:** We understand your vision, goals, required features, target users, and technical requirements.

**Your role:** Clearly articulate what you want built. Answer questions thoroughly. Do not hold back important requirements.

**Output:** A confirmed feature list and project proposal.

**Discovery call terms:**
- In-person meetings: No charge
- Remote calls (first 30 minutes): No charge
- Remote calls beyond 30 minutes: Billed at per-request rates

**Critical rule:** Do not assume any requirement is "obvious." If it is not stated, it is not included.



### Phase 2: Prototype / Design Preview

**What happens:** Based on the discovery, the Provider creates a prototype, video walkthrough, clickable wireframe, or detailed feature breakdown showing exactly what will be built — the UI, UX flow, user journeys, and feature interactions.

**Your role:** Review the prototype carefully. Request changes at this stage — they are free. Once you approve, the scope is locked.

**Output:** Approved prototype or design preview that serves as the definitive project scope.

**Critical rule:** Once you approve the prototype, the scope is locked. Changes requested after approval are Scope Creep and will be billed separately. Do not approve the prototype until you are fully satisfied.



### Phase 3: Development

**What happens:** Active coding begins. The Provider builds your product according to the approved scope.

**Your role:** Remain responsive (within 3 Working Days). Provide any outstanding content, credentials, or decisions promptly. Do not introduce new feature requests during this phase.

**Progress updates:** Weekly updates via WhatsApp or your preferred channel. A staging link will be shared when appropriate.

**Critical rule:** Do not request features, design changes, or integrations during this phase without understanding they constitute Scope Creep and will require a new quote.



### Phase 4: Review & Revisions

**What happens:** The completed MVP is presented to the Client on a staging environment. The Client reviews and provides consolidate feedback.

**Your role:** Test the product thoroughly. Compile all feedback into one document. Distinguish bugs from preference changes.

**Included revisions:** One (1) revision round comprising a maximum of four (4) to five (5) cumulative hours of work, covering only items within the agreed scope.

**Revision window:** Revisions must be submitted within seven (7) calendar days of MVP presentation. After this window, all requests are billed at post-MVP rates.



### Phase 5: Delivery & Deployment

**What happens:** After revisions, the final product is deployed to production. Source code is transferred to the Client. Credentials and documentation are handed over.

**Your role:** Make the final payment. Confirm receipt of code and credentials.

**Output:** Live product, source code repository, deployment documentation.

**Critical rule:** Final payment must be received before production deployment and source code transfer. No exceptions.



### Phase 6: Post-Launch Support

**What happens:** The Provider monitors for Serious Bugs reported by the Client.

**Terms:**
- First 2–3 cumulative hours of Serious Bug fixes: Free (within 30 days of launch)
- Beyond 2–3 hours or after 30 days: Per-request billing applies
- All feature requests, tweaks, and improvements: Billed immediately

**Critical rule:** The launch of your product is a milestone, not the beginning of free unlimited support. Budget accordingly.



## SECTION 4: SCOPE MANAGEMENT

### 4.1 The Scope is a Contract

The approved Project Scope Document — whether a written proposal, a feature list, a prototype, or a video walkthrough — is not a suggestion. It is a binding definition of what will be delivered. Both parties are accountable to it.

### 4.2 How to Identify Scope Creep

Ask yourself before making a request: "Was this shown or listed in the approved scope?"

- **Yes** → It is included. The Provider must deliver it.
- **No** → It is Scope Creep. It requires a new quote.

### 4.3 Common Scope Creep Examples

The following are real examples of Scope Creep that clients frequently attempt to include as "part of the project":

| Client Statement | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Can we add a mobile app too?" | Scope Creep — new project |
| "Can we add a chat feature?" | Scope Creep — not in original scope |
| "Make it also work for a second type of user" | Scope Creep — new user role |
| "While you're at it, add an analytics dashboard" | Scope Creep — separate feature |
| "Can we change the whole design?" | Scope Creep — complete redesign |
| "Add a newsletter subscription too" | Scope Creep — new integration |

### 4.4 How Scope Changes Are Handled

1. Client submits a written change request (email)
2. Provider reviews and responds with estimate within 3 Working Days
3. Client approves or declines in writing
4. If approved: additional deposit may be required before work begins
5. If declined: original scope remains unchanged

### 4.5 The Provider's Right to Refuse

The Provider may decline any change request that: creates architectural conflicts, requires dismantling completed work, falls outside the Provider's stated services, or cannot be accommodated within the current project timeline.



## SECTION 5: REVISION POLICY — DETAILED

### 5.1 What Is and Is Not a Revision

A **Revision** is a correction of something that should have been right based on the agreed scope but wasn't. It is not an opportunity to redesign, rethink, or expand the product.

| Valid Revision (Free within included hours) | Not a Revision (Billed) |
|---|---|
| Feature does not behave as shown in prototype | "I changed my mind about the layout" |
| Navigation link is broken | "Make the colour scheme different" |
| Form does not submit correctly | "Add another field to this form" |
| Page crashes on a specific major browser | "Can we redesign this section?" |
| Mobile layout is broken on all devices | "Change all the fonts" |
| Agreed feature is completely missing | "I want to add a new section" |

### 5.2 How to Submit a Revision Request

All revisions must be submitted using the following format:



**REVISION REQUEST**
**Date:** [DATE]
**Section / Feature:** [WHAT PART OF THE SITE]
**Current Behaviour:** [WHAT IS HAPPENING NOW]
**Expected Behaviour:** [WHAT SHOULD HAPPEN — AS PER AGREED SCOPE]
**Proof:** [SCREENSHOT / VIDEO / LINK IF POSSIBLE]



Vague revision requests such as "it doesn't look right" or "fix the homepage" will not be actioned until a specific, structured request is provided.

### 5.3 Revision Budget Management

- **Included:** 4–5 cumulative hours
- **Tracking:** The Provider tracks time from the moment revision work begins
- **Notification:** Client is notified at 3 hours consumed
- **Exhausted budget:** Provider stops work and notifies Client. Client must approve continuation at post-MVP billing rates

## SECTION 7: DEPOSIT, PAYMENT & LATE PAYMENT POLICY

### 7.1 No Work Without Deposit

No planning, discovery, prototyping, coding, or any other productive work shall begin until the required deposit clears.

### 7.2 Deposit by Client Type

| Client Type | Required Deposit |
|---|---|
| In-person / personally known | 20% of project fee |
| Referred by existing client | 35% of project fee |
| New / cold client | 50% of project fee |

### 7.3 Milestone Payments

After the deposit, remaining payments are split across project milestones:

**Standard 3-Payment Schedule:**
1. Deposit → Work begins
2. 50% milestone → Paid when project is ~50% complete
3. Final payment → Paid before production deployment

**Example (100,000 PKR project, new client):**
- Deposit (50%): PKR 50,000 — before work starts
- Milestone (25%): PKR 25,000 — at 50% completion
- Final (25%): PKR 25,000 — before launch

### 7.4 Late Payment — Step by Step

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Invoice issued | Payment due within 7 calendar days |
| Day 8 | Friendly reminder (email + WhatsApp) |
| Day 14 | Final notice; project work paused immediately |
| Day 21 | Deposit forfeited; full outstanding balance due now |
| Day 30+ | Provider pursues legal remedies under Pakistani law |

### 7.5 Resumption After Late Payment

Payment after suspension does not automatically resume the project at the previous priority. The Client re-enters the Provider's queue and work resumes at the next available slot. Clients whose work has been suspended more than once may be required to pay 50% upfront regardless of client type.
