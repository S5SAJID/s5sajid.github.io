## 1. Purpose and status

**Example — not for use.** This document demonstrates how Pakistan-specific client terms can be presented. It does not create obligations, offer services, or replace a signed agreement. Browsing, copying, or printing this page does not constitute acceptance.

Before publishing operative terms, obtain advice from a qualified Pakistani legal professional who understands the parties, services, and applicable law. No effective date has been assigned to this example.

## 2. Parties and project scope

A completed agreement should identify the service provider and client by their correct legal names and set out how each party can receive formal notices. Do not publish private client details in this public library.

An agreed statement of work should describe:

- The design or development services and deliverables.
- What is expressly excluded from the project.
- Client inputs, dependencies, and responsible contacts.
- Milestones, delivery assumptions, and review criteria.

## 3. Fees and payment

The parties should agree the fee, currency, billing schedule, taxes, expenses, and payment method in writing. Where Pakistani rupees are intended, identify the currency as **PKR** rather than relying on an ambiguous currency symbol.

This example sets no price, deposit, late charge, refund entitlement, or tax treatment. Any operative payment provisions must reflect the actual engagement and applicable requirements.

## 4. Review and changes

A completed agreement should explain how deliverables are submitted, how feedback is consolidated, and what constitutes an agreed revision or a change in scope.

### 4.1. Change requests

Record the proposed change and its effect on cost, scope, and timing. The agreement should specify who may approve a change and how approval is recorded before additional work begins.

### 4.2. Client dependencies

Identify the materials and decisions needed from the client. Explain how delays or unavailable dependencies affect the agreed schedule, without assuming automatic acceptance through silence.

## 5. Confidentiality and information handling

Define confidential information, permitted uses and disclosures, exclusions, and any obligations following termination. Specify appropriate handling of credentials, client materials, and personal information.

This static library is public infrastructure. The country check is a viewing convenience, not a confidentiality safeguard. Never include credentials or confidential project information in these Markdown files.

## 6. Intellectual property

Distinguish client-provided materials, pre-existing tools, third-party components, and newly created deliverables. A completed agreement should state which rights are licensed or assigned, when that occurs, and any conditions.

Address open-source and third-party licence obligations separately. Do not imply that all underlying software or assets can be exclusively transferred.

## 7. Ending the engagement

Set out any grounds and process for termination, notice requirements, treatment of completed and incomplete work, outstanding payments, and handover responsibilities.

The parties should expressly agree any cancellation or refund provisions. This example creates none.

## 8. Disputes and applicable terms

A completed agreement should identify an appropriate dispute-resolution process, governing law, and forum following qualified legal review. This example does not select a province, court, arbitration venue, or limitation of liability.

A visitor’s IP location alone does not determine which laws or contractual terms apply.

## 9. Version and agreement records

An operative agreement should identify the applicable document version and preserve the text accepted by the parties, together with the relevant agreement records.

This website does not collect signatures or record contractual acceptance. Updating a page should not be treated as automatically changing an existing client agreement.
